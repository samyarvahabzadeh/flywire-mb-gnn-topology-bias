# Cognitive review and proposed Molab experiment

## What the experiments established

The preregistered directional prediction was faster acquisition on real wiring. The primary median paired cost was 318.75 trials on the real graph. The subsequent reduced-circuit hypothesis predicted greater near-versus-far selectivity on the real KCγ→MBON11 input mask under local depression, and a larger real-mask advantage under that rule than under Adam. In the exploratory 416-job experiment the first contrast was −0.00387 and the paired interaction was −0.00292 in fractions of conditioned-cue transfer. Matching CS+ response exposed a descriptive learning-rule difference on distant probes. The 12 controls retained 67.1–68.7% of the real pairs and the upstream PN→KC graph remained real in every condition. These results quantify a rule effect under the selected implementation. They supply little evidence for the proposed partner-specific benefit. The comparison also changed the role of CS− feedback, update direction, optimizer and step size together.

## Targeted primary literature review, 24 November 2026

* Hige et al. measured odor-specific depression after odor–dopamine pairing and related cross-odor generalization to overlapping KC responses. That mechanism predates this experiment. [Neuron, 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4674068/)
* Barth et al. found that an explicitly unreinforced similar odor improved behavioral discrimination relative to absolute conditioning. Antennal-lobe inhibitory circuitry also contributed. This specifies an external directional benchmark for the reduced MB model. [J Neurosci, 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24478363/)
* Predictive olfactory learning in Drosophila compared correlation-based plasticity with predictive rules using conditioning experiments. Its error-driven candidate multiplies a signed reinforcement prediction error by presynaptic eligibility. This motivates a matched local comparator. [Nature Communications, 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33762640/)
* A recent whole-brain FlyWire preprint already simulated KC–MBON depression, odor specificity and sequential conditioning. Its results further limit novelty claims for implementing dopamine-dependent odor-specific learning in a connectome. [Asis, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09930)
* Chan et al. found odor-category structure in αβ and partly α′β′ KCs and reported a different pattern for γ KCs. Their intervention concerns PN input structure. Our reduced circuit keeps this input layer fixed and varies γ-KC partners of MBON11. [Cell Reports, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42789392/)

The candidate contribution is a quantitative test of the incremental value of exact presynaptic partner identity for a known cognitive signature under matched plastic capacity. Priority for this exact comparison remains uncertain after this targeted review.

## Proposed test · differential conditioning

Model two 40-PN odors sharing 30 PNs. Counterbalance which predicts punishment and evaluate 32 matched pairs of unseen variants per cue layout, replacing four shared PNs with the same four background PNs in each probe pair. In each training cycle present CS+ with punishment. The second slot is blank for absolute conditioning and the explicitly safe similar CS− for differential conditioning. Freeze the real PN→KC stage, bilateral readout, initial weights of one, [0,1] bounds and 48 cycles. Reuse all 12 previously frozen capacity-matched masks, independent of individual performance. Use 20 fresh cue layouts. Report these as repeated stimuli on one connectome and one control bank.

Let v be the mean fractional depression of active KC inputs across the two MBON11s. The pulse rule updates w by −0.05 x US. The predictive rule uses −0.05 x (US−v). Both see identical cues and punishment sequences. Use immediate activity x, the shared bounded weights and an instantaneous scalar prediction. This is a reduced discrete-time application of predictive plasticity. The full published models contain temporal dynamics and candidate dopamine circuits. Keep the earlier Adam/BCE implementation as a secondary reference with target labels. Its BCE input is 0.25+0.5v. Match the coefficient of the CS+ loss across protocols.

Primary model outcome per mask and rule is the novel positive-family versus negative-family difference in v, averaged over the two assignments of punishment, differential minus absolute training. The test of wiring is the real gain minus the within-layout median of all 12 control gains. The pulse rule predicts exactly zero differential gain because its update ignores the unreinforced cue. This equality is a software and theoretical control. Predict a positive predictive-rule gain, positive real-minus-control gain under that rule and a positive difference of topology effects relative to the pulse rule. Report every component, the raw CS+ and CS− values and graded seed effects including mixed or reverse directions. The CS+-normalized secondary response checks whether conditioning strength accounts for the change. A positive predictive-rule gain shared by all masks supports omission-sensitive updating; it provides no unique advantage to the sampled partners. A negative or tiny contrast limits support. Model responses are proxies for discrimination. Real fly choices, empirically measured odor representations and independent animals remain external validation targets.

## Execution

Build with `python scripts/package_differential.py`. Upload the resulting `dist/differential-molab.zip` to the private Molab notebook's file panel. The ZIP contains the sparse projection, numbered masks, recipe, runner and checksums. Extract it into a dedicated working directory. From there run `python run.py --smoke --output smoke` or `python run.py --output results`. Each seed writes a checksum-protected restart shard. Scientific output is 3,120 condition rows. The 24 smoke rows use a separate seed. Use a persistent output path or copy completed shards and receipts to durable storage between sessions.
